07

Oil, gold, silver

Cross-market — the geopolitical spike in crude and the rate-driven correction in metals.

7.1 Oil

Brent $104.45 (26 Sep), back above $100 in early September for the first time in six weeks. Driver: escalating attacks on Middle East oil facilities and shipping, over 10mb/d of Gulf output shut in, global production down 1.6mb/d m/m to ~100.1mb/d in August; the IEA now expects the Gulf recovery to slip to 2027. A supply-disruption / risk-premium story layered on an underlying oversupply picture — OPEC and the IEA had both been cutting 2026 demand-growth forecasts before the attacks. Re-verify the Russia/Venezuela figures from your notes before citing them — unconfirmed in current reporting.

7.2 Gold & silver — the fuller picture

2025 into early 2026 was a genuine structural bull market: gold's all-time high $5,589 (28 Jan 2026), silver's $121.62 (29 Jan 2026, up ~170% from $38 in July 2025) — structural drivers, not speculation alone:

  • Central-bank buying / de-dollarization: since Russia's ~$300bn in FX reserves were frozen in 2022, central banks (especially BRICS+, now 17.4% of global gold reserves vs. 11.2% in 2019) have diversified reserves away from dollar assets into gold at record pace (~60 tonnes/month average cited for 2026).
  • Silver-specific squeeze: a sixth consecutive year of structural deficit (100–200moz/year shortfall), a COMEX vault drawdown (>33moz in one January week), new AI/electronics industrial demand, and China reclassifying silver as strategic and restricting exports.

Since then both corrected hard — gold to $4,298 (−23%), silver to $64.2 (−47%) — as the Warsh-led hawkish pivot and the September hike pushed real yields up and the dollar firmer (see §2). Frame it as: the structural, multi-year bid is intact; the cyclical driver (real rates, Fed path) just turned against the trade hard enough to dominate short-term. The defensible trade idea is relative-value, not directional: if the hiking cycle is close to done (one more priced), the structural bid should reassert once the rate shock is absorbed — a laddered, scaled re-entry beats chasing the correction or calling an outright fresh long right now. See the India-specific angle in §3.11.
