Analyst notes

What's changed since you last read your notes, and two places where the wiring doesn't quite match the data.

Stale — Warsh is now Fed Chair, not a speaker

Kevin Warsh is the sitting Fed Chair. His hawkish Jackson Hole speech (28 Aug) moved September hike odds from ~55% to ~60% — and the Fed followed through: 16 Sep, +25bp to 3.75–4.00%, first hike since 2023, dot plot pointing to one more this year. This is now your best live example of the "hawkish speech → markets" mechanism (see §2), because you can narrate what actually happened rather than the theory alone.

Two different bond stories were merged into one

The US Treasury tripled its long-bond buyback size (to $6bn/operation, 10–30Y sector) — a liquidity-support operation funded by new issuance, not "reducing debt," driven by fading foreign demand (China and Japan both trimming UST holdings). The US 30Y sits near a 19-year high, ~5.2–5.3% — nowhere close to 7%. The "sub-7%" figure in your notes is almost certainly India's 10Y G-sec (see §3.1), which the RBI is fighting to hold under 7% via OMO sales — it just broke that level. Keep the three levers separate: Treasury (issuance/liquidity), Fed (policy rate), RBI (defending a yield).

Oil's direction is right, the driver is inverted

Brent at $104.45 is a geopolitical supply-disruption story (Middle East attacks on facilities and tankers, >10mb/d of Gulf output shut in) — not an OPEC+ cut. If anything, OPEC and the IEA had been cutting 2026 demand outlooks going in, i.e. fundamentals pointed to oversupply. Your Russia-output and Venezuela figures don't show up in current reporting — don't cite them as hard numbers; lead with the Middle East disruption story, which is well documented. Detail in §7.1.

Gold and silver already corrected — use this as your worked example

Both are down hard from January 2026 peaks: gold $5,589 → $4,298 (−23%), silver $121.62 → $64.2 (−47%). Proximate cause: the same Warsh/Fed hawkish turn above. The structural bid (de-dollarization, central-bank buying) is intact underneath — the trade right now is "a rate shock overwhelmed the structural bid," not "these are still ripping." Full picture in §7.2.
